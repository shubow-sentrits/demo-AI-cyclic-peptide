Constrained peptides couple covalent topology, stereochemistry, three-dimensional structure,
conformational dynamics and developability. We review physicochemical rule embedding across
chemical-language, target-conditioned structural, geometric, atom-bond, ensemble and property-
guided generative models, distinguishing generated molecular objects from downstream inference
and predictions from prospective evidence. We identify limitations in chemical coverage, applicability,
uncertainty and pipeline attribution, and routes towards identity-preserving, environment-aware and
experimentally calibrated design.
Constrained peptides occupy a productive region of chemical space between
small molecules and large biologics1. Their size and conformational plasti-
city can support recognition of broad, shallow, or discontinuous surfaces,
including protein-protein interaction interfaces, enzyme exosites, and
receptor surfaces that are often difﬁcult for conventional small molecules to
address

tion, D-amino acid incorporation, and related modi ﬁcations can reduce
conformational entropy, improve proteolytic resistance, and tune the bal-
ance between preorganization and adaptability. These features make mac-
rocyclic and otherwise constrained peptides attractive modalities for targets
that are incompletely served by either classical medicinal chemistry or large
protein therapeutics
3.
This versatility also makes the design problem highly coupled. The
object being proposed is not merely an amino acid sequence, but a che-
mically speciﬁed scaffold with covalent connectivity, stereochemistry, one
or more accessible conformations, and an environment-dependent physi-
cochemical proﬁle

side-chain lactams, and N-methylate d heterochiral macrocycles impose

Generative modeling has expanded the scale at which peptide-like
candidates can be proposed under sequence, topology, target, or property
constraints10. Early rule-based generators emphasized enumerability and
formal validity, whereas recent approaches increasingly place chemical or
physicochemical constraints within molecular notation, topology-aware
structural representations, geomet ric or atom-bond generation, con-
formational sampling, and property-guided optimization. These studies
broaden the accessible design space, but the claims they support depend on
the chemical object represented, the stage at which a constraint acts, and the
endpoint that is actually benchmarked or measured.
Generic architecture labels are therefore less informative than the
molecular object represented and the point at which a rule changes the
generative distribution. Foundational work on Bayesian neural networks
provides methodological context for predictive uncertainty
11,w h i l ee a r l y
stochastic treatments of reinforcement learning provide general context for
reward-driven updating12; neither is treated here as constrained-peptide-
speciﬁc evidence. Post hoc ﬁltering remains valuable for triage and valida-
tion, but it is separated from constraints that directly shape sampling.
Recent reviews and perspectives have surveyed therapeutic-peptide
generative models13, computational and artiﬁcial intelligence-driven stra-
tegies for cyclic-peptide discovery10, and generative AI in peptide-based
drug design14. Broader work on large language models in drug discovery
provides related architectural context 15. The present scope is narrower:
absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET)-
and developability-related models are discussed only when they shape
constrained-peptide generation, guide candidate selection, deﬁne property

Generated objects and representation adequacy
Meaningful comparison begins with the molecular object a method actually
produces. We distinguish seven levels: a residue sequence; a covalent graph
that speciﬁes monomers and inter-monomer bonds; a stereochemically
resolved molecular identity; a single three-dimensional conformer; a
population-weighted conformational ensemble; a set of environment-
conditioned ensembles; and a targ et-bound complex. The levels are
information-bearing rather than interchangeable. A parseable sequence
does not establish linkage geometry, a valid graph does not establish low
strain, a plausible conformer does not establish population weights, and an
unbound ensemble does not establish a binding mode or afﬁnity.
Representation adequacy is ther efore endpoint-dependent, not a
monotonic function of detail. Serialized notations can support large-scale
chemical enumeration when topology and stereochemistry are unambig-
uous, whereas permeability, chameleonicity, intramolecular hydrogen
bonding and recognition often dependon conformational populations and
environment

in the generated object, inferred by the model, reconstructed by a down-

Results for canonical head-to-tail macrocycles do not establish performance
on disulﬁde-rich, stapled, side-chain-linked, heterochiral, N-methylated or
multicyclic systems. Claims of modality-level generalization should be
stratiﬁed by topology, ring size, linkage chemistry, stereochemical compo-
sition, noncanonical-residue burden and assay context. Cross-class gen-
eralization should be claimed only when performance is evaluated on pre-
speciﬁed held-out topology or chemistry classes and reported per class with
explicit denominators, uncertainty intervals and failure rates; otherwise,
conclusions should remain within the demonstrated chemical scope.
Design challenges and modelable rules
Design variables rarely change independently in a constrained peptide16.A
residue substitution can alter charge, hydrophobicity, hydrogen-bonding
capacity, secondary-structure preference, and protease susceptibility; in a
macrocycle, the same edit can also change ring strain, closure geometry,
side-chain packing, intramolecular hydrogen-bond networks, and access to
a target-compatible pose. A string ge nerator may preserve peptide-like
composition while missing disul ﬁde registration, lactam spacing, staple
geometry, stereochemistry, or multicyclic linker compatibility
5. Conversely,
a structure generator may produce a plausible bound conformation while
remaining insensitive to permeability, solubility, puriﬁcation difﬁculty, or
metabolic liability17. The relevant search space is therefore chemically het-
erogeneous, topology-dependent, and environment-sensitive, rather than
reducible to a single objective.
Residue chemistry supplies the local physicochemical vocabulary for
this search
16. Charge distribution, hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors,
hydrophobic and aromatic content, N-methylation, D-residue substitution,
proline placement, and local amphipathicity can inﬂuence solubility, serum
stability, protease resistance, cell p enetration, and target recognition 18.

Topology adds a distinct set of rules once residues are connected into a
constrained scaffold5. Head-to-tail amides, side-chain lactams, disulﬁdes,
thioethers, hydrocarbon staples, backbone-to-side-chain bonds, and mul-
ticyclic crosslinkers are generally not recoverable from sequence adjacency
alone. Modelable variables include monomer identity, bond order, linkage
atoms, anchor compatibility, ring size, stereochemistry, crosslink multi-
plicity, and reaction feasibility. These variables can be represented through
constrained vocabularies, graph anno tations, linkage masks, geometry
restraints, chemically valid decoding operations, or topology-conditioned
sampling. The important distinction is whether topology is merely
expressible in the molecular representation, selected during generation, or
enforced by the sampling and decoding process. A formally cyclic string or
valid graph may still correspond to str ained closure geometry, incorrect
stereochemistry, or an impractical synthetic route.
Cyclization alone does not resolve the conformational problem
21.
Constrained peptides often populate multiple low-energy states, and the
conformation that engages a target may differ from those favored in aqueous
or membrane-like environments22. Intramolecular hydrogen bonds, cis-
trans amide isomerization, side-chain rotamers, and polar exposure can
shift with solvent and binding context
23. Relevant modelable rules therefore
include closure-compatible torsions, chirality, cis-trans state, intramolecular
hydrogen-bond patterns, rotamer com patibility, solvent exposure, and
ensemble populations. These features can be incorporated as internal-
coordinate constraints, ensemble-conditioned objectives, solvent- or state-
speciﬁc descriptors, or physical reﬁnement and validation steps. A single
closed conformer can support a geometric plausibility claim, but it is limited
evidence for aqueous, membrane-competent, and target-bound
populations.
Developability introduces another layer of coupled and assay-speciﬁc
endpoints. Permeability, solubility, proteolytic stability, metabolic liability,
hemolysis, toxicity, and synthetic t ractability are not independent of
topology or conformation. Cyclic-peptide permeability models have been
compared across scaffold and representation choices
24, while applicability-
domain analysis remains essential for judging when such predictions are
meaningful
20. Related endpoints include peptide half-life, which has been
modeled from blood-stability data and from enzymatic-cleavage features
transferred across species and organs25,26, and hemolytic propensity, which
can be addressed with neural-network safety classiﬁers27. Models may use
measured or predicted endpoints as rewards, preferences, posterior gui-
dance, acquisition criteria, or ﬁlters. Docking energies, predicted perme-
ability, half-life estimates, and safety scores remain surrogate outputs;
improvements in these scores are co mputational changes rather than
measured binding, permeability, stability, safety, pharmacokinetics, or
therapeutic beneﬁt, particularly outside a deﬁned applicability domain
20 or
near activity cliffs28. Prospective absorption, distribution, metabolism, and
excretion studies show why retro spective accuracy alone is insuf ﬁcient29.
Assay identity is part of the endpoint: PAMPA and Caco-2 assays capture
different aspects of transport
30, MDCK-MDR1 adds an efﬂux-aware cellular
context31, and bulk live-cell measurements provide another route to
experimental permeability labels32.
Rules can intervene at several points in a generative work ﬂow.
Representation determines what can be expressed; conditioning speciﬁes
target, topology, closure, property, or ensemble context; and decoding can
restrict monomers, atoms, bonds, ster eochemistry, or ring size. During
training or sampling, denoising, ﬂow matching, reward optimization,
preference-aligned updating33, posterior guidance, or active learning can
bias the proposed distribution before candidates are ﬁnalized. Evaluation
tools generally act later through scoring,ﬁltering, relaxation, docking, ret-
rospective benchmarking, uncertaintyestimation, or experimental valida-
tion. Energy and design frameworks 34,c y c l i c - p e p t i d ed o c k i n g
benchmarks35, peptide tertiary-structure benchmarks that include disulﬁde-
cyclic examples36, force-ﬁeld assessments of cyclic-peptide ensembles37,a n d
permeability-predictor comparisons24 illustrate how evaluation can reveal
plausibility and limitations without necessarily changing the initial sampling
distribution. Evidence is therefore interpreted at the level actually bench-
marked or measured: computational generation, structural assessment,
biochemical testing, cell-based activity, transport measurements, and pro-
spective synthesis are treated as distinct endpoints.
A practical distinction follows. Generation-time embedding includes
rules in the molecular representatio n, conditioning va riables, decoding
grammar, denoising or ﬂow trajectory, loss, reward, preference objective,
posterior guidance, or optimization path. Evaluation-time use includes
scoring, ﬁltering, relaxation, ranking, r etrospective benchmarking, and
experimental validation. A third role is conceptual: medicinal-chemistry or
biophysical principles may motivate interpretation without being imple-
mented in the model. Reporting these roles separately clariﬁes whether a
rule changes the sampling distributionor is applied after candidate proposal.

Figure 2 summarizes the chronological and methodological landscape, and
Table2 provides the corresponding model-level comparison. The taxonomy
is organized by the molecular object principally generated and by the

as Transformer, diffusion model, orﬂow model. Several systems span more
than one capability; their placement re ﬂects the dominant contribution

retrospective benchmarking, predicted property optimization, structural
validation, biochemical binding or cellular activity, permeability measure-

Five recurrent families are useful for organizing the surveyed literature.
Sequence, notation, and latent-space methods operate on serialized mole-
cular descriptions or sequence-level latent variables. Topology-aware target-
conditioned systems generate or op timize bound cyclic backbones and
sequence-structure solutions. Constraint-conditioned geometric and atom-
bond models place closure or covalent chemistry directly in residue- or
atom-level generation. Ensemble methods sample distributions of accessible
conformations. Developability-guided methods alter or select candidate
populations according to property preferences.
These categories are complementary rather than mutually exclusive. A
notation model may also use reinforcement learning, a topology generator
may include auxiliary property gui dance, and a permeability-focused
workﬂow may combine prediction, selection, and limited generation.
Therefore, the placement of each method in Fig.2 and Table 2 indicates its
dominant contribution, not an exclusive capability boundary. CycloPs

Sequence, notation, and latent-space generation
Sequence and notation models gain scale by treating a constrained peptide
as a writable chemical language. HELM-GPT

HELM-GPT
39 occupies the bounded-vocabulary end of this design
space. HELM keeps monomer identityand inter-monomer bonds explicit,

learning

prior, but it also limits exploration to the available vocabulary. Within the
demonstrated workﬂow, reported property gains are predictor outputs, and
native three-dimensional structure generation or measured af ﬁnity and
permeability were not reported. The method is therefore most useful as an
auditable chemical-language layer.
PepINVENT
40 exposes more of each monomer ’s chemical structure
through CHUCKLES. Masked in ﬁlling can edit canonical residues and

connectivity, while retaining a peptide-oriented grammar. Relative to aﬁxed
monomer token, the SMILES-derived description permits ﬁner chemical
edits but increases the burden of vale nce, stereochemical, and synthetic
checks. Reinforcement-learning scores can guide the policy; based on the
reported evidence, the principal advance is representationalﬂexibility rather
than solved geometry or measured developability.
The comparison is therefore not simply HELM versus CHUCKLES,
but curated monomer semantics versus more exposed submonomer
chemistry. HELM-GPT makes the available building blocks and con-
nectivity readily auditable, whereas PepINVENT gives the generator greater
freedom to modify molecular structure. Neither notation alone is sufﬁcient
to establish stereochemical ﬁdelity after decoding , feasible three-
dimensional closure, a low-energy ensemble, or a target-compatible pose.
These boundaries reﬂect the generated object rather than a failure of che-
mical notation.

Topology-aware target-conditioned structure design
Topology-aware target-conditioned design offers a comparatively direct
route from a generated object to an experimentally testable binding
hypothesis. Earlier structure-based pipelines established complementary
precedents: exhaustive design of ordered macrocycles48, structure-guided
anchor extension into enzyme active sites49, rapid template matching with

not uniformly native to their represe ntations. In the models emphasized

bound geometry rather than being checked only after a linear binder has
been proposed. Their binding evidence is interpreted within that context
and is not generalized to permeability, stability, or broader pharmacology.

Constraint-conditioned geometric and atom-bond
generation
A second line of work makes closure or covalent chemistry the principal
control variable. CP-Composer73 uses composable type and distance con-
straints, GeoCycler 74 aligns diffusion with topology-speci ﬁcr e w a r d s ,
APCyc75 predicts cyclization variables jointly with sequence and structure,

stochastic differential equation. Their shared contribution is generation-

validation is predominantly computational.
CP-Composer decomposes stapled, head-to-tail, disulﬁde, and bicyclic
designs into composable residue-typ e and pairwise-distance constraints
within a geometric latent diffusion model. The underlying geometric design
setting is related to PepGLAD
54, while DiffPepBuilder55 serves as a specia-
lized comparison for disulﬁde-constrained design. The constraints alter the
denoising trajectory and can be combined at inference, enabling zero-shot
application beyond the linear-peptidetraining distribution. Closure success
and selected molecular dynamics analyses support geometric plausibility,
while the outputs remain canonical and no synthesis, binding, or perme-
ability assays are reported. Within this scope, the method provides a clear
demonstration of composable geometric control.
GeoCycler shifts the same class of closure signal from inference-time
guidance toward training-time reward alignment. Its type-gated reward
activates distance shaping only when compatible anchor identities are
present, reducing misleading gradientsfrom chemically incompatible pairs.
Improvements in pass@k closure metrics support stronger alignment of the
learned distribution to prescribed topologies. Based on the reported evi-
dence, difﬁcult multicyclic cases remain more challenging, and synthesis,
binding, and developability were not evaluated experimentally; the main
advance is reward-aligned topology control.
APCyc treats cyclization type and linkage sites as variables predicted
jointly with sequence and three-dimensional structure. Extended residue
tokens, a linkage matrix, compatibilitymasks, and equivariant updates make
topology an explicit part of denoising, while Bayesian posterior guidance can
bias selected property surrogates. Th e central contribution is automated
topology-sequence-structure generation. Property-guided results are best
interpreted as an auxiliary comput ational demonstration because the
objectives are proxies, experimenta l calibration of uncertainty was not
reported, and no prospective synthesis or assay was included.
CpSDE moves further toward chemical explicitness by representing
cyclization with an atom-bond graph and incorporating that graph into a
harmonic stochastic differential eq uation through a Laplacian-derived
prior. Routed sampling alternates all-atom structural denoising with residue
prediction and supports several backbone- and side-chain-linked closure
patterns in one framework. Computational energy, afﬁnity, and molecular
dynamics analyses suggest useful stru ctural behavior, while the residue
router remains canonical and the demonstrated treatment of stereo-
chemistry and clashes is limited. CpSDE is therefore best viewed as an atom-
bond generative advance whose broader noncanonical and experimental
scope remains to be demonstrated.

Developability-guided optimization
When generation is coupled to property scores, the central question

Future directions
The most tractable advances lie at identity-preserving interfaces where one
capability hands off to another. Progress should therefore be judged not only
by adding modules, but by whether chem ical identity, stereochemistry,
topology, conformational state, assay context, and uncertainty remain
traceable across the design work ﬂow. Useful next steps are those that
broaden topology, connect conformational states to measured properties,
and use experiments to challenge rather than merely con ﬁrm model
rankings.
Topology selection should move inside stereochemically explicit che-
mical grammars rather than remain a short menu of predeﬁned closures.
Existing stereogenic, reward-aligned, automated-linkage, and atom-bond
generative approaches provide partial ingredients
72,74–76, but the next step is
to let target and property context choose among head-to-tail, side-chain,
disulﬁde, thioether, stapled, and multicyclic solutions under shared validity
rules. Residue-level planning should therefore be coupled to atom-level
valence masks, chiral constraints, a nd reaction-compatible linkage tem-
plates. Conservative validity gates would be essential, because a larger
topology space is useful only if gener ated candidates remain chemically
interpretable.
Environment-conditioned ensembles provide a direct bridge between
geometry and developability.Current internal-coordinate,ﬂow-matching,
mixed-chirality diffusion, and autoencoder methods offer useful ensemble
primitives
77,78,83,84, but water, membrane-like media, and target-bound states
are not yet commonly linked within one objective. Future models could
preserve a shared molecular identity across condition-speci ﬁc ensemble
heads, with population losses anchored by simulation, NMR, hydrogen-
exchange, crystallographic, or permea bility-related observables. Because
conformational sampling canm i s sr e l e v a n ts t a t e si nﬂexible macrocycles
113,
agreement among several independent physical signals would be more
convincing than reliance on one energy model.
Environment-conditioned ensembles also impose a throughput con-
straint that is absent from single-state objectives. Cost scales with candidate
count, environmental states, conformers per state and the physical eva-
luations applied to each conformer; repeated relaxation or enhanced sam-
pling is therefore dif ﬁcult to reconcile with unrestricted library-scale
search
83. Learned conformer generators and conditioned Boltzmann
models82 can amortize part of this cost, but they inherit assumptions from
the reference sampler, forceﬁeld and training domain37.
A tiered design loop can allocateﬁdelity selectively. Two-dimensional
or language-model predictors provide inexpensive triage, applicability and
uncertainty gates determine when thos e estimates are credible, learned

Outlook
Constrained-peptide generation is moving from valid-object construction
toward coordinated models of chemical grammar, target geometry, covalent
topology, conformational state, and therapeutic objective. The useful
question is not which generic architecture is used, but where a physico-
chemical rule changes the distribution : in representation, conditioning,
denoising, ﬂow, decoding, preference opti mization, or experimental
feedback.
The ﬁve model families are complementary rather than interchange-
able. Sequence, notation, and latent-space methods provide scalable che-
mical language; target-conditioned design contributes structural and binding
hypotheses; geometric and atom-bond models encode or guide closure and
connectivity; ensemble methods r epresent state populations; and
developability-guided systems negotiate predicted or measured property
trade-offs. Convergence will be most meaningful when each component
carries its validity conditions, evidence level, and uncertainty into the next.
Translation calls for a stricter standard than computational plausibility.
A generated constrained peptide becomes more therapeutically informative
when its chemistry is unambiguous, its relevant states support the proposed
mechanism, its property estimates are calibrated within domain, and
synthesis and assays test the same claim. Selective prospective validation is
therefore part of the design loop rather than an interchangeableﬁnal score.
The central opportunity is to generate fewer candidates with stronger
internal coherence. Topology, chemistry, conformational ensemble, target
engagement, developability, and synthetic route are most useful when they
form one testable hypothesis. Physicochemical rule embedding offers both a
framework for building such system s and an audit trail for judging the
claims they produce.
